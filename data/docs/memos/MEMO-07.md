## Section 1 Purpose

This training note is for new adjusters, service staff and underwriters who need to navigate the policy wording documents of Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd. The policy wording is the definitive contractual document that governs what is covered, what is excluded, what waiting periods apply and how claims are to be processed. Accurate reading of the policy wording is a fundamental skill for anyone assessing a claim or advising a Policyholder.

A common source of errors is using the wrong policy wording version, misreading sub-clauses in isolation, or applying the terms of one product to a claim under a different product. This training note explains the structure of the wording documents, how to identify the right version and how to avoid the most common reading mistakes.

## Section 2 Structure of the Policy Wording

Health Gold and Health Silver each have their own policy wording documents. Health Gold has two active wording versions — Health Gold Policy Wording 2024 (HG-WORD-2024) and Health Gold Policy Wording 2025 (HG-WORD-2025) — and Health Silver has one (HS-WORD-2025). All three follow the same clause structure, though the values differ between products and versions.

2.1 Clause 1 (Definitions): the opening clauses define every key term used in the wording — Hospital, Insured Person, Policy Schedule, Pre-Existing Disease and so on. Always read the applicable definition before applying a term. The same word may carry a precise technical meaning under the Policy Wording that differs from everyday usage.

2.2 Clause 2 (Benefits): these clauses define what is covered — in-patient hospitalisation, day care procedures, domiciliary hospitalisation, road ambulance and any other covered benefit. Each clause specifies conditions the admission or treatment must meet to qualify.

2.3 Clause 3 (Sum Insured and Sub-limits): this clause states the sum insured options and the sub-limits that apply to specific procedures. Sub-limits reduce the payable amount for certain treatments.

2.4 Clause 4 (Waiting Periods): these clauses define the initial waiting period, specific disease waiting periods and the Pre-Existing Disease waiting period. The specific durations differ between wording versions; always use the version applicable to the date of loss.

2.5 Clauses 5–10 cover exclusions, claim procedures, cashless and reimbursement processes, room rent, renewal and portability, and grievances respectively.

## Section 3 Version Control and Common Mistakes

3.1 Identifying the right version: the applicable policy wording version is determined by the date of loss, not the policy inception date or renewal date. Use the date of loss to identify whether HG-WORD-2024 or HG-WORD-2025 applies to a Health Gold claim. If in doubt, check the products.csv reference file, which maps effective date ranges to wording documents.

3.2 Reading sub-clauses in context: each sub-clause (e.g. 4.2.1) must always be read in the context of the main Clause above it (e.g. Clause 4.2). Sub-clauses do not stand alone. Reading a sub-clause without the parent clause can lead to an incorrect interpretation.

3.3 Citing clause references: when writing assessment notes or rejection communications, always cite the full clause reference — for example, "Clause 4.2 of HG-WORD-2025" — not just the clause number on its own. This helps both the Policyholder and any grievance reviewer understand which wording version and clause was applied.

3.4 Cross-referencing riders: where the Policy Schedule shows a rider, the rider document must be consulted alongside the main wording. Some riders modify specific clauses of the wording; always check whether a rider applies before finalising the assessment.
