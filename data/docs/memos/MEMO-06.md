## Section 1 Purpose

This memo informs claims adjusters and service staff of updated standards for the scanning and digital submission of claim documents. As the Company moves toward a fully digital claims workflow, the quality of scanned documents submitted by Policyholders and hospitals directly affects the speed and accuracy of claim assessments. Poor-quality scans result in deficiency notices, delays and additional customer contact — all of which can be avoided by ensuring documents are scanned correctly before submission.

The updated standards described in this memo apply to all documents submitted in support of health insurance claims, including discharge summaries, itemised bills, diagnostic reports, prescriptions, identity documents and any other document required under SOP-DOCS-01. Staff receiving document submissions — whether by email, portal upload or in person — must use the standards in Section 2 to assess whether a submitted document is acceptable.

## Section 2 Updated Scanning Standards

Staff and customers must adhere to the following quality standards for all scanned claim documents.

2.1 Legibility: every scanned document must be fully legible. All text, signatures, dates and amounts must be readable without zooming or enhancement. Documents where any part of the text is blurred, cut off, overexposed or underexposed must be returned to the submitter for rescanning.

2.2 Completeness: the full document must appear in the scan. For multi-page documents such as hospital bills and diagnostic reports, all pages must be included. A partial scan that omits one or more pages is treated as an incomplete document and will trigger a deficiency notice.

2.3 Format: the preferred file format for all submissions is PDF. Where image files are submitted (such as JPEG or PNG), the resolution must be sufficient to ensure all text is clearly legible. Files must not be compressed to the point where quality is degraded.

2.4 Single combined file: where multiple documents are submitted for a single claim, they should be combined into a single PDF file wherever possible, clearly labelled with the claim reference number.

2.5 Colour: documents that contain colour-coded information — such as pathology reference ranges or hospital letterheads — should be scanned in colour, not in black and white.

## Section 3 Transition and Support

These standards take effect immediately for all new claim submissions. Claims already registered before this memo was issued are not affected; deficiency notices for those claims should be handled under the previous standards.

3.1 Service staff receiving document submissions that do not meet the standards in Section 2 must raise a deficiency notice in accordance with SOP-DOCS-01, citing the specific standard that was not met and explaining how the document should be resubmitted.

3.2 Staff who need guidance on communicating scanning requirements to customers may refer to SOP-COMMS-01 for language guidance and to the customer helpline team for support with specific cases.

3.3 Any technical issues with the online document upload portal should be reported to the IT helpdesk promptly so they can be resolved without delay to claim processing.
