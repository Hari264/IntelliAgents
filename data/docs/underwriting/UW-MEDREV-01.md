## Section 1 Purpose

This guideline defines the process for reviewing medical reports submitted by proposers or Insured Persons in support of underwriting decisions for health insurance policies issued by Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd. Medical reports are requested where the proposal form discloses a health condition, where mandatory pre-policy tests reveal a finding requiring further review, or where a sum insured enhancement is requested by a person with a known health history. This guideline applies to individual, family floater and group health proposals and renewals, and must be read alongside UW-RISK-01, UW-PED-01 and UW-MEDTEST-01.

## Section 2 Reading Medical Reports

2.1 Medical reports received by the underwriting team must be reviewed within a reasonable period of receipt. The underwriter must check: (a) that the report is dated and signed by a registered medical practitioner with the relevant qualification; (b) that the report addresses the specific condition or finding for which it was requested; (c) that the diagnosis, current treatment details and clinical status of the condition are clearly stated; and (d) that the report is consistent with the health declaration on the proposal form.

2.2 Common types of medical reports include: physician's letters confirming diagnosis and current management; specialist consultation notes; diagnostic test reports such as blood panels, imaging reports and ECG findings; and procedure notes. Each type provides a different level of clinical detail and must be read in conjunction with the others where more than one type is submitted.

2.3 Where a report uses medical terminology that the underwriter cannot interpret without specialist knowledge, the underwriter must not make assumptions. The report must be escalated to the Company's Medical Officer as described in Section 3.

2.4 The underwriter must note whether the condition described in the report is stable and well-managed, or active and evolving. Stable, managed conditions with documented treatment may support acceptance at standard or rated terms. Active, poorly controlled conditions require escalation.

## Section 3 Escalation to the Medical Officer

3.1 The underwriter must refer a medical report to the Company's Medical Officer for a formal opinion in any of the following situations: (a) the report discloses a serious or complex medical condition that requires specialist clinical interpretation; (b) the report contains findings that are inconsistent with the health declaration, suggesting possible non-disclosure; (c) the report reveals a condition that may affect the applicability of waiting periods, exclusions or the risk class under UW-RISK-01; or (d) the underwriter is uncertain whether the condition described meets the criteria for a loading, exclusion or decline.

3.2 The escalation must be made in writing and must include: the nature of the condition; the specific question being referred to the Medical Officer; the relevant sections of the medical report; and any other contextual information from the proposal file.

3.3 The Medical Officer's written opinion must be attached to the proposal file and must inform the final underwriting decision. Where the Medical Officer's opinion differs from the underwriter's preliminary view, the Medical Officer's opinion takes precedence, and the basis for the final decision must be documented accordingly.

## Section 4 Recording the Review

4.1 The underwriter must record in the proposal file: the date the medical report was received; the date it was reviewed; the key clinical findings noted; whether the report was escalated to the Medical Officer; and the underwriting decision taken as a result of the review, with supporting reasons.

4.2 Where the medical report resulted in a loading or an exclusion endorsement, the specific condition and the basis for the underwriting action must be documented clearly so that the rationale is available for future renewals and for any claim that may subsequently arise.

4.3 All medical reports received form part of the confidential proposal file and must be stored securely. Access is limited to underwriting staff, the Medical Officer and others with a documented need to know.

4.4 Medical report reviews are subject to quality audit under SOP-QUALITY-01 and may be sampled by the Senior Underwriter to ensure consistency of interpretation and decision-making across the team.
