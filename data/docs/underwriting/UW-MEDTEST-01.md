## Section 1 Purpose

This guideline defines the pre-policy medical test requirements for individual and family health insurance proposals at Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd. It specifies when medical tests are mandatory, which tests are required by age band, the validity period for test results, and the circumstances in which test requirements may be waived.

Pre-policy medical tests enable the Company to assess the health status of proposers accurately, supplement the information provided on the proposal form, and ensure that the risk class assigned under UW-RISK-01 reflects the proposer's actual health profile. Tests must be conducted at a laboratory or diagnostic centre approved by the Company, or at any centre whose results are acceptable to the Company, as notified to underwriters from time to time. The cost of mandatory pre-policy medical tests is borne by the Company.

## Section 2 Tests by Age Band

The requirement for pre-policy medical tests depends on the age band of the proposer as defined in UW-AGE-01 and on the risk profile disclosed on the proposal form.

2.1 Proposers in age bands 1 and 2 (18 to 45 years): pre-policy medical tests are not mandatory for proposers in these age bands, unless the risk profile or a declared Pre-Existing Disease triggers a discretionary test requirement as described in Section 2.3.

2.2 Proposers in age band 3 and above (46 years and above): pre-policy medical tests are mandatory for all proposers aged 46 and above, regardless of their declared health status. A proposal from a proposer in this age group must not proceed to issuance without satisfactory test results on file.

2.3 Discretionary tests below age 46: the underwriter may require medical tests for a proposer below age 46 in any of the following circumstances: (a) the proposer has declared one or more Pre-Existing Diseases on the proposal form, and the nature of the condition warrants verification; (b) the proposer is applying for a sum insured at the higher end of the available range and the risk profile is Class B or above; or (c) the proposer's BMI as declared suggests a risk that cannot be adequately assessed from the proposal form alone.

2.4 The specific panel of tests to be conducted by age band and risk profile is set out in the Company's Medical Test Schedule, which is maintained by the actuarial and medical advisory team and updated periodically. Underwriters must use the current Medical Test Schedule. As a general guide, the standard test panel for age band 3 and above includes a complete blood count, fasting blood glucose, lipid profile, kidney function tests, liver function tests, ECG and blood pressure measurement.

## Section 3 Test Validity

Medical test results submitted in support of a health insurance proposal are valid for a period of 6 months from the date of the test. Results older than 6 months must not be accepted, and the proposer must be asked to undergo a fresh test at the Company's expense.

3.1 The 6-month validity period runs from the date on which the test was conducted, as stated on the laboratory report. The date of the report or the date the report was issued is not the relevant date.

3.2 Where a proposer undergoes medical tests and then delays the submission of the proposal such that the tests expire before inception, the proposer must undergo a fresh test before the policy can be issued.

3.3 For renewals, medical test results conducted within 6 months before the renewal date are acceptable. The Company does not require tests at every renewal unless the insured person moves into age band 3 at renewal, or unless the renewal underwriting assessment identifies a fresh health concern.

3.4 Tests conducted for one product or sum insured option may be used to support a different product or sum insured option within the same insurer, provided they were conducted within the validity period and the test panel is adequate for the new product's requirements.

## Section 4 Waiver Rules

A mandatory medical test requirement may be waived only in the following circumstances and subject to approval by the Senior Underwriter.

4.1 Portability from another registered insurer: where a proposer transfers under the NIR's portability framework and has undergone medical tests with the prior insurer within 6 months before the transfer date, and the prior insurer's test results are made available to the Company, the Senior Underwriter may waive the requirement for a fresh test.

4.2 Group health policies: for group health proposals, the mandatory test requirement for individual members aged 46 and above may be modified by the terms of the group underwriting arrangement, subject to the approval of the Senior Underwriter and the minimum group test requirements in the Company's group health guidelines.

4.3 Administrative difficulty: where a proposer residing in a remote area has genuine difficulty accessing an approved test centre, the Senior Underwriter may accept results from the nearest accredited laboratory, provided the test panel is equivalent. This waiver must be documented on the proposal file.

4.4 A test waiver must never be granted merely for administrative convenience. All waivers must be documented with the reason and the approving Senior Underwriter's name and signature.
