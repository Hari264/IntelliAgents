## Section 1 Purpose

This guideline defines the occupational risk classification framework used by underwriters at Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd. when assessing group health insurance proposals. It assigns occupational classes based on the nature of the employer's business activities and the working environment of the employees to be covered, and specifies the premium loading applicable to each class.

Occupational risk classification reflects the elevated health risk that arises from certain work environments, including exposure to hazardous substances, physical injury risk, or high-stress or physically demanding conditions. The classification must be determined at the group level based on the primary business activity of the employer and the occupational profile of the workforce to be insured. Individual members of the group are not separately classified unless their individual roles differ materially from the group's primary occupational class.

## Section 2 Occupational Classes

The Company uses four occupational classes for group health insurance. The loading applicable to each class is applied to the base premium before any age-band or health risk loading.

Class 1 — Office and Clerical: applies to employers and employee groups whose primary work involves office-based, administrative, clerical, managerial, professional or information technology activities. The working environment is a standard office setting with no material exposure to physical, chemical or environmental hazards. No occupational loading applies to Class 1.

Class 2 — Light Warehousing, Retail and Logistics: applies to employers and employee groups in light warehousing operations, retail settings, distribution centres and last-mile logistics. This class includes employees who operate forklifts or pallet handlers in standard warehouse settings, retail staff, and delivery personnel on standard road routes. A loading of 10% applies to Class 2.

Class 3 — Heavy Manufacturing, Chemical Handling, Fireworks and Fuel: applies to employers and employee groups in heavy manufacturing, industrial processing, chemical handling and storage, fireworks production and storage, fuel depot operations and petroleum product handling. Employees in this class are exposed to significant hazards including chemicals, heat, combustible materials and heavy machinery. A loading of 25% applies to Class 3.

Class 4 — Mining and Explosives: applies to employers and employee groups in underground and open-cast mining, quarrying and the handling, storage or transportation of explosives and detonators. Proposals in this class must be referred to the Chief Underwriter. Class 4 is declined by default; issuance may be considered only with the express approval of the Chief Underwriter and on terms to be determined case by case.

## Section 3 Warehousing and Logistics

Warehousing operations vary considerably in their risk profile. This section provides guidance on the classification of the most common warehousing categories. In each case, the underwriter must obtain written answers to the fire-safety questions listed in this section before assigning a class.

3.1 Cold storage warehouses (food, pharmaceutical and dairy products): these facilities store perishable goods at low temperatures using refrigeration systems. The primary risks are mechanical failure, refrigerant handling and general slip-and-fall hazards. Cold storage operations are generally classified as Class 2. Fire-safety questions: Does the facility have a functional automatic fire suppression system? Are compressed gas cylinders stored in a designated, ventilated area? Is there a current fire safety certificate from the relevant local authority?

3.2 Textile warehouses (fabric, garments, yarn and apparel): these facilities store combustible textile materials and are at elevated fire risk owing to the density and flammability of stored goods. Textile warehouses are generally classified as Class 2. Fire-safety questions: What is the maximum stack height of stored materials? Is the facility equipped with sprinklers or fire suppression? Are all electrical fittings in storage areas of industrial grade?

3.3 FMCG warehouses (packaged consumer goods, food products and beverages): these facilities handle high volumes of packaged goods with moderate fire risk. Generally classified as Class 2. Fire-safety questions: Is there a current fire NOC (no-objection certificate)? Are aerosol products stored separately from other goods? What fire-fighting equipment is available on site?

3.4 E-commerce fulfilment centres (general merchandise and household goods): these large facilities handle diverse categories of goods and have variable risk profiles depending on the mix of products. Generally classified as Class 2 for standard general merchandise. Fire-safety questions: Does the centre store any goods classified as flammable or hazardous under applicable Indian law? Is there a documented fire emergency plan with regular fire drills? Are aisles and emergency exits kept clear at all times?

## Section 4 Unlisted Occupations

Any occupation or storage type not explicitly listed in this guideline must be referred to the Chief Underwriter. Do not assign a class by analogy.

When referring an unlisted occupation, the underwriter must submit to the Chief Underwriter: (a) a description of the employer's primary business activity; (b) a description of the working environment of the employees to be covered; (c) the number of employees in the group; (d) any information available on the nature and severity of occupational hazards present; and (e) the underwriter's preliminary assessment of the most analogous listed class, for information only.

The Chief Underwriter will review the referral and issue a written classification decision within 10 working days. That decision is binding for the proposal in question and will be circulated to the underwriting team as a precedent where the Chief Underwriter determines it has wider applicability.

No group health policy for an unlisted occupation may be issued, priced or declined until the Chief Underwriter's classification decision has been received. The underwriter must not communicate a provisional premium or indicative terms to the employer or broker before the classification decision is obtained.
