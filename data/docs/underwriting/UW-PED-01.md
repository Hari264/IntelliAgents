## Section 1 Purpose

This guideline sets out the requirements for the declaration of Pre-Existing Diseases (PEDs) at the proposal stage for individual and family health insurance policies issued by Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd. It also defines the acceptable forms of evidence used to support PED declarations and the consequences of non-disclosure.

Accurate PED declaration is the foundation of the health underwriting process. It enables the Company to assess risk correctly, apply appropriate waiting periods in accordance with the applicable Policy Wording, and identify cases where a loading or exclusion endorsement is warranted. Underwriters are responsible for reviewing PED declarations critically and for requesting supporting evidence where the declaration appears incomplete or inconsistent with the proposer's other responses on the proposal form.

## Section 2 Declaration Requirements

Every proposer must declare all Pre-Existing Diseases on the proposal form at the time of application.

2.1 Definition for declaration purposes: a Pre-Existing Disease for the purposes of the proposal declaration is any condition, ailment, injury or disease for which the proposer received a medical diagnosis or treatment at any time during the 48-month period immediately before the proposed policy start date, as defined in Clause 1.2.1 of the applicable Policy Wording.

2.2 Content of declaration: for each declared condition, the proposer must state: (a) the name or description of the condition; (b) the date of first diagnosis; (c) the name of the treating physician or specialist; (d) the treatment received, including the name of any medication being taken currently or taken within the 48-month period; and (e) the current status of the condition (active, managed, resolved).

2.3 Completeness obligation: the proposer's obligation is to disclose all conditions that meet the definition in Clause 2.1, not only those that the proposer believes are material. The underwriter must not accept a declaration that lists only "significant" conditions without clear guidance to the proposer on the scope of the obligation.

2.4 Duty of care: underwriters must draw the proposer's attention to the declaration requirement at the time of application and must ensure that the proposal form is signed by the proposer confirming the accuracy and completeness of the declarations made.

## Section 3 Acceptable Evidence

Where a proposer has declared one or more Pre-Existing Diseases, the underwriter may request supporting medical evidence to assess the nature, severity and current status of the condition. Acceptable forms of evidence include:

3.1 A letter or certificate from the proposer's treating physician or specialist confirming the diagnosis, the date of first diagnosis, the current treatment plan and the present clinical status of the condition.

3.2 Original or certified copies of discharge summaries from any hospitalisation relating to the declared condition within the 48-month period before the proposed policy start date.

3.3 Original or certified copies of diagnostic test reports (blood tests, imaging, ECG, biopsy reports) that document the condition or its progression.

3.4 Prescription records or pharmacy dispensing records demonstrating the medications prescribed for the declared condition and the period of treatment.

3.5 Where the declared condition has been resolved, a physician's letter confirming the date of resolution and confirming that no further treatment or monitoring is required.

The underwriter may request a pre-policy medical examination at the Company's expense where the nature or severity of the declared condition cannot be adequately assessed from documents alone.

## Section 4 Non-disclosure Consequences

Non-disclosure of a Pre-Existing Disease — whether at inception, at renewal or upon endorsement — is treated as a serious matter that may affect the validity of the Policy.

4.1 Where non-disclosure is identified at the claims stage: the claims adjuster must refer the matter to the underwriting team using the process described in SOP-PEDVER-01. The underwriting team will investigate whether the non-disclosure was material — that is, whether the Company would have issued the policy on the same terms had the condition been declared.

4.2 Where non-disclosure is found to be material: the underwriting team may take one or more of the following actions: (a) apply the PED waiting period as per the applicable Policy Wording (Clause 4.3) retrospectively; (b) add an exclusion endorsement for the undisclosed condition; (c) refer the case to the Senior Underwriter for a decision on cancellation or voidance of the policy in accordance with applicable law and NIR guidelines, as summarised in CIR-03.

4.3 Where non-disclosure is found to be immaterial — for example, a minor resolved condition that would not have affected the underwriting decision — the underwriting team may decide to note the non-disclosure without adverse action on the policy.

4.4 All non-disclosure findings and the actions taken must be documented in the underwriting file and reported in the monthly underwriting review.
