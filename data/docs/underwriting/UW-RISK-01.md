## Section 1 Purpose

This guideline defines the health risk classification framework used by underwriters at Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd. when assessing individual and family health insurance proposals. It sets out the risk classes available, the criteria for placing a proposal in each class, and the underwriting action associated with each class.

The purpose of risk classification is to ensure that premiums are commensurate with risk, that the Company's portfolio remains sustainable, and that cover is extended to as wide a population as possible within acceptable risk parameters. Risk classification must be applied consistently and objectively, using the information disclosed on the proposal form and any supporting medical evidence obtained during the underwriting process. Underwriters must not decline or load a proposal on the basis of factors that are not medically or actuarially justified.

## Section 2 Risk Classes

Health insurance proposals are assessed into one of four risk classes. The applicable loading or underwriting action for each class is specified in the Company's rating manual; specific loading percentages are not reproduced in this guideline.

Class A — Standard Risk: the proposer presents no significant adverse health history, no active chronic conditions requiring regular medication, a BMI within the normal range for their age and height, and no significant family history of hereditary conditions. Cover is offered at standard premium rates with no exclusion endorsements.

Class B — Rated Risk: the proposer has one or more conditions that can be underwritten with a premium loading or a specific exclusion endorsement. Examples include well-managed hypertension controlled by medication, well-controlled type 2 diabetes without organ complications, a resolved non-malignant condition with no recurrence in the past two years, or a BMI that is above the standard range but below the referral threshold. The loading or exclusion applied is determined by the rating manual.

Class C — Sub-standard Risk: the proposer has multiple conditions, an uncontrolled chronic disease, or a recent serious illness that falls outside the parameters for Class B. Proposals in this class require review and approval by the Senior Underwriter before a decision is issued.

Class D — Declined: the medical profile of the proposer indicates a risk level that cannot be underwritten at any loading. Examples include active malignancy under treatment, advanced organ failure, or a recent major cardiac event within the period specified in the rating manual. Proposals in this class are declined with written communication to the proposer.

## Section 3 Lifestyle Factors

In addition to medical history, the following lifestyle factors are assessed during underwriting and may affect the risk class assigned to a proposal.

3.1 Tobacco use: current smokers and those who have ceased smoking within the past two years are assessed under the criteria in the rating manual. The form of tobacco use (cigarette, pipe, chewing tobacco) is documented.

3.2 Body mass index (BMI): a BMI above the Class A threshold moves the proposal to Class B for BMI assessment purposes. BMI is calculated using the height and weight declared on the proposal form. Where there is reason to doubt the declared figures, a medical examination may be requested.

3.3 Occupation: high-risk occupations as classified in UW-OCC-01 may require an occupational loading in addition to the standard health risk assessment. The occupational class must be determined before the overall premium is calculated.

3.4 Family history: a first-degree family history of hereditary conditions (including certain cardiac conditions, hereditary cancers and genetic disorders) is noted on the proposal file. It does not automatically result in a higher risk class but is a factor considered alongside the proposer's own medical history.

3.5 Physical activity and sports: participation in hazardous or adventure sports as listed in the Company's exclusion schedule is noted. A specific exclusion endorsement may be applied or an additional premium charged.

## Section 4 Referral Rules

Certain proposals must be referred to the Senior Underwriter or Chief Underwriter and must not be decided at standard underwriter level.

4.1 Any proposal where the proposer's medical history cannot be clearly assigned to a single risk class using the criteria in Section 2.

4.2 Any proposal for a proposer aged 66 or above with a declared Pre-Existing Disease, which requires Senior Underwriter review.

4.3 Any proposal where the proposer requests a sum insured of ₹25,00,000 and the risk class is Class B or above.

4.4 Any group health proposal where the group includes more than 20% of members in Class C or above, as determined by the aggregate risk assessment.

4.5 Any proposal for an occupation or risk profile not covered by existing guidelines, in accordance with UW-OCC-01 Section 4.
