## Section 1 Purpose

This guideline defines the process for reviewing proposal forms submitted by proposers for health insurance policies issued by Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd. The proposal form is the primary document on which the underwriting decision is based. An incomplete or inconsistent form may lead to an incorrect underwriting decision and may expose the Company to non-disclosure issues at the claims stage. This guideline applies to new proposals and to renewal proposals where a fresh health declaration has been requested, across individual, family floater and group health policies.

## Section 2 Completeness Checks

2.1 On receipt of a proposal form, the underwriter must verify that all mandatory fields are completed: (a) the full name, date of birth and address of the Policyholder and each proposed Insured Person; (b) the sum insured option requested; (c) the declaration of all health conditions, including any conditions diagnosed or treated in the period preceding the proposal; (d) the name and dosage of any prescribed medication currently being taken by any proposed Insured Person; (e) the signature of the Policyholder and, where the product requires it, each Insured Person; and (f) the date on which the declaration was signed.

2.2 The underwriter must confirm that the health declaration section has been answered in full — that every question has been answered either affirmatively or negatively, and that no question has been left blank or marked as "not applicable" without an accompanying explanation.

2.3 The underwriter must verify that the age of each proposed Insured Person is consistent with the date of birth stated on the form, and that the correct age band under UW-AGE-01 has been assigned for premium rating.

2.4 For proposed Insured Persons aged 46 and above, the underwriter must flag the proposal for mandatory pre-policy medical tests in accordance with UW-MEDTEST-01 before the policy is issued.

## Section 3 Inconsistencies

3.1 The underwriter must check for internal inconsistencies in the form, including: (a) a declared health condition that does not correspond to any prescribed medication listed; (b) a declaration of "no prior hospitalisation" combined with a disclosed chronic condition that would ordinarily require hospitalisation; (c) a sum insured option that appears disproportionate to the proposer's disclosed health profile without explanation; and (d) a discrepancy between the declared date of birth and the age stated elsewhere on the form.

3.2 Where a declaration appears inconsistent with other information provided, the underwriter must not resolve the inconsistency by assumption or inference. The specific inconsistency must be queried in writing with the proposer or intermediary before an underwriting decision is made.

3.3 Where the inconsistency relates to a health condition that may constitute a Pre-Existing Disease, the underwriter must review the matter under UW-PED-01 before proceeding to the underwriting decision. In such cases, additional medical evidence may be required under UW-MEDREV-01.

## Section 4 Return to Proposer

4.1 A proposal form must be returned to the proposer or intermediary where: (a) mandatory fields are blank or incomplete; (b) the health declaration section has not been completed in full; (c) the form is unsigned or undated; or (d) the information provided is internally inconsistent and cannot be resolved from the documents accompanying the proposal.

4.2 On returning the form, the underwriter must issue a written notice clearly identifying each section or question that requires completion or correction, and stating the reason each item is required. The notice must be courteous in tone and must invite the proposer to resubmit.

4.3 A returned proposal form does not constitute a decline of insurance. The proposer retains the right to resubmit a corrected form. The underwriting process begins afresh on receipt of the corrected form.

4.4 All returns and the specific reasons for them must be recorded in the underwriting system. Where the same form is returned on more than one occasion for the same deficiency, the underwriter must escalate to the supervisor to determine whether further action is appropriate.
