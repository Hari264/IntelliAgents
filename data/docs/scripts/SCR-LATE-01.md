## Section 1 When to Use

Use this script when a claim has been delayed, declined or placed on hold because the notification or document submission was not received within the required timeframe.

CL-108 — Claim notified after the allowed notification time: the Company was notified of the hospitalisation after the deadline specified in Clause 6.1 of the Policy Wording — at least 48 hours before a planned admission, or within 24 hours of an emergency admission.

CL-112 — Reimbursement documents submitted after 30 days of discharge: the claim documents were received more than 30 days after the date of discharge, contrary to Clause 6.2.1 of the Policy Wording.

Before calling, retrieve the claim reference {claim_id}, the rejection code {rejection_code}, the date of notification or document submission, and the required deadline. Check the claims management system to confirm whether the adjuster has already reviewed the case for condonation or is awaiting the customer's explanation. Do not decline solely on the basis of late notification or late submission until the customer has been given the opportunity to explain.

## Section 2 Customer Script

**Script for CL-108 — Late notification:**

"Good [morning / afternoon / evening]. May I please speak with {customer_name}?

Thank you. This is [your name] from Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd. regarding your health insurance claim number {claim_id}. I am calling about the notification we received for your hospitalisation — claim reference {rejection_code}.

Under Clause 6.1 of your Policy Wording, planned hospitalisations must be notified to us at least 48 hours before admission, and emergency hospitalisations must be notified within 24 hours of admission. Our records show that notification for your claim was received after the required deadline.

Before we finalise the assessment of your claim, I would like to understand if there was a specific reason why notification could not be made within the required period. For example, if the severity of your condition prevented notification, that is something our team can take into account.

Could you tell me why notification was delayed? I will note your explanation and pass it to our team for review. We will then write to you with our decision on whether the late notification can be condoned.

Is there anything else I can help you with?"

**Script for CL-112 — Late document submission:**

"Good [morning / afternoon / evening]. May I please speak with {customer_name}?

Thank you. This is [your name] from Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd. regarding your health insurance claim number {claim_id} under code {rejection_code}.

Under Clause 6.2.1 of your Policy Wording, reimbursement claim documents must be submitted within 30 days of your date of discharge. Our records show that your documents were received after this deadline.

Before we finalise the assessment, I would like to understand whether there was a specific reason for the delay — for example, if you were unwell, or if obtaining certain documents took longer than expected.

Could you share the reason for the late submission? I will note it and refer it to our team. We will then write to you with our decision on whether the delay can be condoned.

If the late submission is condoned, your claim will proceed to normal assessment. If it cannot be condoned, we will explain the reason in writing and you will have the option to raise a grievance.

Thank you for your cooperation."

## Section 3 Do and Do Not

DO: always give the customer the opportunity to explain the reason for the delay before the claim is finalised; note the explanation in the claims management system and pass it to the adjuster for review; inform the customer that a written decision will follow; mention the grievance route if the delay cannot be condoned.

DO NOT: tell the customer the claim will definitely be declined because of the late notification or late submission — the condonation process must be followed; promise that the delay will be condoned; decline the claim on this basis alone during the call — the final decision must be made by the adjuster and communicated in writing; use a dismissive tone about the customer's explanation.
