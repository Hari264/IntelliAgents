## Section 1 Background

The National Insurance Regulator (NIR) issued this circular to strengthen grievance redressal standards across the health insurance sector and to address deficiencies identified in the disclosure standards applied by insurers at the proposal stage. The circular addresses concerns that policyholders are not given adequate information about their policy terms before inception, and that grievances are not being resolved within required timelines.

This document is an internal summary of the NIR circular for the guidance of claims, service and underwriting staff at Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd. The requirements in this summary apply to all health insurance policies administered by the Company. Underwriters should pay particular attention to the disclosure requirements in Section 3, which affect the proposal and inception process. Claims and service staff should pay particular attention to Sections 2 and 4.

## Section 2 Grievance Timelines

The NIR prescribes mandatory timelines for the redressal of all policyholder grievances relating to health insurance policies.

Acknowledgement: every written grievance received by the insurer must be acknowledged within 3 working days of receipt. The acknowledgement must confirm receipt of the grievance, provide a unique grievance reference number, identify the officer or team responsible for handling the matter, and give an indicative timeline for resolution.

Resolution: every grievance must be resolved and a written resolution communicated to the Policyholder within 14 days of receipt of the grievance. Where the complexity of the grievance genuinely prevents resolution within 14 days, the insurer must inform the Policyholder in writing before the 14-day period expires, stating the reason for the extension and a revised resolution timeline. A grievance is not considered resolved merely because the insurer has communicated a decision; the resolution communication must address the substance of the grievance and provide specific reasons.

The insurer must maintain a grievance register recording all grievances received, the actions taken and the outcomes. This register is subject to periodic inspection by the NIR.

## Section 3 Disclosure at Proposal Stage

The NIR requires insurers to ensure that adequate disclosures are made to proposers at the time of application and before policy inception.

Proposer obligations: every proposer must disclose all Pre-Existing Diseases on the proposal form. A Pre-Existing Disease for this purpose is any condition for which the proposer received a medical diagnosis or treatment at any time during the 48-month period immediately before the policy start date. The declaration must include the diagnosis date and details of treatment received. A proposer who fails to disclose a Pre-Existing Disease — whether intentionally or inadvertently — may face rejection of a related claim or cancellation of the policy following investigation by the Company's underwriting team.

Insurer obligations: before the policy is issued, the insurer must provide the Policyholder with: the full policy wording; a clear statement of the key waiting periods applicable to the product; the definition of Pre-Existing Disease as it applies to the policy; and the process for making a claim, including notification requirements and document submission timelines.

These disclosures must be made in writing and must be accessible to a lay policyholder.

## Section 4 Escalation to Ombudsman

If a policyholder's grievance is not resolved by the insurer within the prescribed 14-day period, or if the policyholder is dissatisfied with the insurer's resolution, the policyholder has the right to escalate the matter to the Insurance Ombudsman.

The Insurance Ombudsman is an independent dispute resolution body established by the NIR to resolve disputes between policyholders and insurers at no cost to the policyholder. The Ombudsman has jurisdiction based on the Policyholder's place of residence or the location of the insurer's office where the policy was issued.

The insurer must co-operate fully with the Ombudsman's inquiry, providing all documents and records requested within the specified timeframe. An Ombudsman award, if issued, is binding on the insurer. The insurer must communicate the contact details of the relevant Insurance Ombudsman's office to all policyholders upon request and must include them in every written grievance response. Failure to co-operate with the Ombudsman is itself a regulatory breach and may result in sanctions.
