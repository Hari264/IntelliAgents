## Section 1 Background

The National Insurance Regulator issued this circular to address the widespread practice of insurers issuing multiple, successive requests for documents, thereby delaying the settlement of legitimate claims and causing avoidable distress to Policyholders. The circular sets out the NIR's expectations for how insurers should identify, request and handle documents needed to process health insurance claims.

This document is an internal summary of the NIR circular for the guidance of claims and service staff at Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd. For claim settlement timelines, refer to CIR-01. For rejection communication requirements, refer to CIR-02. For grievance timelines, refer to CIR-03. The Company's internal document checklist is in SOP-DOCS-01.

## Section 2 Documents the Insurer May Request

2.1 The NIR confirms that insurers are entitled to request documents that are genuinely necessary to assess the validity and quantum of a claim. All document requests must be proportionate to the nature and complexity of the claim.

2.2 Documents that may be requested include: completed claim forms; original hospital bills and receipts; discharge summaries; diagnostic reports; prescriptions; identity verification documents (KYC); and any other document that is reasonably required to verify the clinical facts, the identity and eligibility of the Insured Person, or the amount claimed.

2.3 The NIR cautions against requesting documents that are not reasonably available to the Policyholder or that place a disproportionate burden on the claimant. Where a document is unavailable due to circumstances outside the Policyholder's control, the insurer must consider whether a credible alternative can be accepted.

2.4 Reimbursement claim documents must be submitted by the Policyholder within 30 days of discharge from the Hospital, as required by the applicable Policy Wording (Clause 6.2.1). The insurer must acknowledge receipt promptly and issue a deficiency notice for any missing items without undue delay.

## Section 3 Avoiding Repeated Requests

3.1 The NIR requires that all document requests be consolidated into a single notice issued as early as possible in the claim process. An insurer must not issue a second or subsequent document request for items that could reasonably have been identified and requested in the initial notice.

3.2 Repeated document requests extend the settlement clock, because the period during which a request is outstanding does not count toward the settlement timeline. Repeated requests are therefore both a service failure and a contributor to settlement timeline breaches, as described in CIR-01.

3.3 The Company's process for raising deficiency notices is set out in SOP-DOCS-01. The deficiency notice must be issued as a single consolidated communication listing all missing documents. This requirement also applies to requests made during the assessment stage, where the adjuster identifies a need for additional information.

## Section 4 Handling Missing Documents

4.1 Where the Policyholder is unable to provide a mandatory document — for example, because an original bill has been lost — the NIR expects the insurer to consider whether alternative evidence can be accepted. The insurer must not automatically treat the claim as unsupported if the Policyholder can provide credible alternative evidence such as a certified copy or an affidavit.

4.2 Where a document request is outstanding, the insurer must communicate clearly to the Policyholder the consequence of non-submission — that is, that the claim cannot be assessed until the document is received — and must give the claimant a reasonable period to respond before the file is closed.

4.3 The settlement clock is paused from the date of a document request to the date of receipt of the last outstanding document. Insurers must not misuse this provision by requesting unnecessary documents to extend the settlement period beyond what is genuinely required.

4.4 Policyholders who dispute the insurer's document requirements have the right to raise a grievance as described in CIR-03 and in Clause 10.1 of the applicable Policy Wording. The insurer must acknowledge all such grievances within 3 working days and resolve them within 14 days.
