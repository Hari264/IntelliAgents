## Section 1 Background

The National Insurance Regulator (NIR) issued this circular in response to a significant volume of policyholder complaints alleging that insurers declined claims without providing adequate or specific reasons. The NIR identified cases where policyholders were unable to exercise their rights of grievance or appeal because rejection letters cited only vague grounds or generic references to policy terms, without identifying the specific clause or the factual basis for the decision.

This document is an internal summary of the NIR circular for the guidance of claims and service staff at Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd. Compliance with the standards set out in this summary is mandatory. A rejection communication that does not meet these standards may be treated as deficient by a grievance authority or the Insurance Ombudsman and may result in an adverse finding against the Company.

## Section 2 Written Communication Requirement

Insurers are required to communicate the reasons for a claim rejection or partial disallowance to the Policyholder in writing. Verbal notification by telephone or in person does not satisfy this obligation on its own.

The written rejection communication must be issued within 15 days of receipt of the last necessary document from the claimant. The 15-day period is measured from the date the last necessary document is received by the insurer, not from the date the internal assessment decision is made. Where a claim is partially disallowed — meaning that some expenses are approved and others are not — the partial disallowance must be addressed in a written communication with the same specificity as a full rejection.

Electronic communication by email satisfies the written communication requirement provided the Policyholder has registered an email address with the insurer and has not objected to electronic correspondence. Physical post remains an acceptable channel and may be used as a backup where electronic delivery cannot be confirmed.

## Section 3 Content of Rejection Letter

The NIR requires that every rejection or partial disallowance letter contain the following elements.

3.1 Identification: the claim reference number, policy number, Insured Person's name, date of loss and name of the treating hospital, so that the Policyholder can unambiguously identify the claim to which the letter relates.

3.2 Specific ground for rejection: a clear statement of the ground or grounds for rejection in plain language, expressed so as to be understandable to a lay policyholder. The use of internal codes or unexplained technical terms without further explanation is not acceptable.

3.3 Policy clause reference: the specific clause of the applicable Policy Wording under which the rejection is made. The clause number and the heading of the clause must both appear in the letter. Where the rejection is on the basis of a waiting period, the applicable wording version must also be identified so that the Policyholder knows which version of the Policy Wording has been applied.

3.4 Amounts: the claimed amount and, in a partial disallowance, the amount approved and the amount disallowed, with a line-by-line explanation of each disallowed item.

3.5 Grievance rights: the Policyholder's right to lodge a grievance with the insurer's Grievance Redressal Officer and the contact details for doing so.

## Section 4 Customer Rights

The NIR confirms the following rights for every policyholder who receives a rejection or partial disallowance of a health insurance claim.

Right to a written explanation: the Policyholder is entitled to a full written explanation of the rejection, as described in Section 3, at no charge and within 15 days of the last necessary document.

Right to grievance: the Policyholder may challenge the rejection through the insurer's internal grievance redressal mechanism. The insurer must acknowledge the grievance within 3 working days and resolve it within 14 days of receipt. Where the insurer upholds the rejection following internal review, it must communicate this decision in writing with specific reasons.

Right to escalate to the Insurance Ombudsman: if the Policyholder is not satisfied with the insurer's internal resolution, or if the grievance is not resolved within the required timeframe, the Policyholder has the right to escalate to the Insurance Ombudsman having jurisdiction over their place of residence. The insurer must inform the Policyholder of this right in every rejection communication and must provide the Ombudsman's contact details upon request.

The insurer may not charge any fee for processing a grievance or for providing documents requested in connection with a grievance.
