## Section 1 Background

The National Insurance Regulator (NIR) issued this circular to establish uniform and enforceable timelines within which registered insurers must settle health insurance claims. The circular addresses longstanding policyholder complaints about delays in claim settlement and aims to ensure that insurers process claims with both accuracy and timeliness.

This document is an internal summary of the NIR circular for the guidance of claims, service and underwriting staff at Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd. All staff involved in claim processing must be familiar with the timelines set out in this summary and must apply them to every health insurance claim handled by the Company. Where an internal SOP specifies a more stringent timeline than this summary, the more stringent timeline applies. Non-compliance with NIR settlement timelines may result in interest liability, regulatory findings and reputational harm to the Company.

## Section 2 Settlement Timelines

The NIR requires all registered insurers to settle health insurance claims within 30 days of receipt of the last necessary document from the claimant. The "last necessary document" is the final document required by the insurer to complete the assessment of the claim, including any document requested by the insurer after initial submission.

Where the insurer reasonably requires an investigation before a settlement decision can be made, the maximum permissible settlement period is 45 days from receipt of the last necessary document. The decision to treat a claim as requiring investigation must be recorded, with reasons, in the claims management system.

The settlement timeline commences on the date the last necessary document is received. If the insurer issues a request for additional documents, the clock for the 30-day or 45-day period is restarted from the date on which the requested document is received, provided the request was genuinely necessary to assess the claim. Insurers may not use repeated document requests to artificially extend the settlement period.

## Section 3 Cashless Timelines

For cashless hospitalisation at a network hospital, the NIR prescribes separate and more stringent timelines for pre-authorisation and final discharge authorisation.

Pre-authorisation decision: the insurer must communicate the pre-authorisation decision to the treating hospital within 1 hour of receiving the complete pre-authorisation request. The request is considered complete once the insurer has received the provisional diagnosis, proposed treatment or procedure, estimated length of stay and estimated cost. Where any of this information is missing, the clock commences when the missing information is provided.

Discharge authorisation: the insurer must issue the final discharge authorisation to the treating hospital within 3 hours of receiving the hospital's final bill. The final bill is the itemised bill covering all charges incurred during the admitted period.

These cashless timelines run from the moment the complete request or final bill is logged in the insurer's system. Prompt logging of all incoming cashless requests is therefore essential to ensure accurate TAT monitoring and regulatory compliance.

## Section 4 Interest on Delay

Where the insurer fails to settle a claim within the prescribed period — 30 days for standard claims or 45 days for claims under investigation — the insurer is liable to pay interest to the claimant on the outstanding claim amount.

The NIR prescribes the interest rate at 2% per annum above the prevailing bank rate for the entire period of delay, calculated from the day after the settlement deadline to the date of actual payment to the claimant.

Interest is calculated on the amount of the claim ultimately found to be payable and must be included automatically in the settlement payment where a TAT breach has occurred. It is the responsibility of the adjuster to identify any breach and to notify the finance team so that the interest amount is calculated and included before the claim is closed. A claim must not be closed as fully settled without the interest component where a breach has taken place. Repeated TAT breaches are reportable to the NIR and may attract regulatory sanctions against the Company.
