## Section 1 What Is Day Care Cover Under Health Gold

Q: What is a Day Care Procedure under Health Gold?

A: A Day Care Procedure is a surgical procedure or treatment that, owing to advances in medical technology, can be completed within less than 24 hours without the need for an overnight in-patient admission. Under the applicable Policy Wording (Clause 2.3), the 24-hour minimum admission requirement that applies to standard in-patient claims does not apply to Day Care Procedures. The procedure must appear on the Company's Day Care Procedure list, which is maintained by the Company's medical team and updated periodically.

Q: Does any procedure performed in less than 24 hours automatically qualify as a Day Care claim?

A: No. The procedure must appear on the Company's Day Care Procedure list. A procedure that is completed in less than 24 hours but is not on the list does not qualify under the Day Care provision. The adjuster must check the current list at the date of the procedure before assessing the claim. If a procedure is described using a non-standard name, the adjuster must verify whether it maps to a listed procedure before making a decision.

Q: What waiting periods apply to Day Care claims?

A: The same waiting period provisions that apply to an equivalent in-patient claim for the same condition also apply to a Day Care Procedure claim. For specific waiting periods applicable to a given procedure, refer to the applicable Policy Wording rather than to this FAQ. Portability credit under Clause 9.2 applies to Day Care waiting periods in the same way as to in-patient waiting periods.

## Section 2 What Is Domiciliary Hospitalisation Under Health Gold

Q: What does domiciliary hospitalisation mean?

A: Domiciliary Hospitalisation under Health Gold (Clause 2.4) means medical treatment received by an Insured Person at their home in lieu of admission to a Hospital. For the cover to apply, the treating physician must certify in writing that hospitalisation was not possible or was medically inadvisable due to the nature of the Insured Person's condition.

Q: Is there a minimum period for domiciliary hospitalisation to qualify?

A: Yes. The period of continuous home-based treatment must exceed 3 consecutive days. Treatment that lasts 3 consecutive days or fewer does not qualify under the domiciliary hospitalisation provision.

Q: Does domiciliary cover include routine nursing visits or physiotherapy?

A: Routine outpatient consultations, home nursing visits for ongoing chronic condition management and standard physiotherapy sessions do not qualify as domiciliary hospitalisation unless the treating physician certifies that the specific criteria in Clause 2.4 of the applicable Policy Wording are met.

## Section 3 What Documents Are Required for Day Care and Domiciliary Claims

Q: What documents are needed for a Day Care claim?

A: In addition to the mandatory documents listed in Clause 6.2 of the applicable Policy Wording (and in SOP-DOCS-01), Day Care claims require: operation notes or procedure notes confirming the procedure performed and its duration; the admission and discharge record showing both times on the same date; and, where a medical device was used, the original invoice and the device identification sticker. The adjuster must also check whether any applicable sub-limit or waiting period applies under the Policy Wording.

Q: What documents are needed for a domiciliary hospitalisation claim?

A: Domiciliary claims require the standard documents plus: a written physician certification confirming that hospitalisation was not possible or was medically inadvisable, with the specific clinical reasons; daily treatment records for the entire period; prescriptions for all medicines administered; and nursing notes where a registered nurse provided care. All documents must be submitted within 30 days of the conclusion of the home treatment period.

## Section 4 Common Questions About Day Care and Domiciliary Cover

Q: Can a cashless facility be used for Day Care procedures?

A: Yes. Where the Day Care Procedure is performed at a Network Hospital and pre-authorisation is obtained, the cashless facility may be used. The standard pre-authorisation process in SOP-CASHLESS-01 applies.

Q: If domiciliary treatment follows a hospital stay, are both covered?

A: In-patient hospitalisation and domiciliary hospitalisation are assessed separately under their respective clauses. If a discharge from hospital is followed by qualifying domiciliary treatment, both may be assessed under the applicable Policy Wording. The two periods cannot both be claimed as the same hospitalisation event for sub-limit purposes.

Q: Who maintains the Day Care Procedure list and how often is it updated?

A: The Day Care Procedure list is maintained by the Company's medical team. It is accessible in the claims management system. The list may be updated from time to time and the version applicable at the date of the procedure governs. Policyholders may request the current list from the Company.
