## Section 1 General Policy Terms

Hospital: a registered establishment that maintains at least ten in-patient beds, has a qualified medical practitioner on duty at all times, maintains qualified nursing staff under registered supervision, has a fully equipped operation theatre, and keeps daily patient records accessible to the Company.

Insured Person: each individual named in the Policy Schedule who is covered under the health insurance policy during the Policy Period.

Policy Schedule: the schedule forming part of the policy, issued by the Company to the Policyholder, setting out the names of the Insured Persons, the sum insured, the Policy Period, the premium paid, and any riders or endorsements attached to the policy.

Network Hospital: a hospital that has a valid cashless arrangement with the Company or its designated third-party administrator at the time of the Insured Person's admission. The network list is published by the Company and is accessible through the helpline or website.

Continuous Cover: uninterrupted health insurance cover, whether under the current policy or under a prior insurer's policy, including portability credit granted under the Portability clause of the applicable Policy Wording.

Policyholder: the individual or entity in whose name the policy is issued and who is responsible for payment of premium. The Policyholder may also be an Insured Person under the policy.

## Section 2 Medical and Clinical Terms

Pre-Existing Disease: any condition, ailment, injury or disease that has been diagnosed, or for which the Insured Person received medical advice or treatment, at any time during the 48-month period immediately before the date on which the policy first incepted for that Insured Person.

Arthroplasty: a surgical procedure in which a diseased or damaged joint is replaced in whole or in part with a prosthetic implant. Also referred to as joint replacement surgery. The term covers all joint replacement procedures regardless of the joint involved, including total and partial replacements.

Medically Necessary Treatment: treatment, procedure or supply that is required for the diagnosis or direct treatment of a condition; is in accordance with generally accepted medical standards in India; is not primarily for the convenience of the patient or physician; and is of the minimum necessary intensity and cost to achieve the clinical outcome.

In-patient Hospitalisation: formal admission to a Hospital as a registered in-patient for a minimum continuous period of 24 hours for the purpose of receiving Medically Necessary Treatment.

Day Care Procedure: a surgical procedure or treatment that, owing to advances in medical technology, is completed within less than 24 hours of hospitalisation and is listed on the Company's Day Care Procedure list applicable at the date of the procedure.

Domiciliary Hospitalisation: medical treatment received at the Insured Person's home for a continuous period of more than 3 days, where the treating physician certifies that hospitalisation was not possible or was medically inadvisable.

## Section 3 Claims and Coverage Terms

Sum Insured: the maximum aggregate amount the Company will pay for all covered claims made by an Insured Person in a Policy Year. Options under Health Gold are ₹3,00,000; ₹5,00,000; ₹10,00,000; and ₹25,00,000. The sum insured resets at the start of each Policy Year upon renewal.

Sub-limit: a monetary cap within the sum insured that applies to a specific procedure or benefit. Examples under Health Gold include cataract surgery at ₹40,000 per eye and joint replacement surgery at ₹3,00,000 per joint. Amounts paid under a sub-limit reduce the available sum insured.

Cashless Facility: an arrangement under which the Insured Person receives treatment at a Network Hospital without paying upfront; the Company settles eligible expenses directly with the hospital, subject to pre-authorisation and the terms of the Policy Wording.

Reimbursement: the process by which the Insured Person pays the hospital directly and submits original bills to the Company for reimbursement of eligible expenses. Available at both network and non-network hospitals.

Pre-authorisation: the Company's prior written approval for cashless treatment, issued in response to a request from the hospital before or at the time of admission. Pre-authorisation is not a guarantee of final claim settlement.

Proportionate Deduction: a reduction applied to associated medical expenses (excluding pharmacy, implants and consumables) when the Insured Person occupies a room category above the eligible room rent limit. The reduction is calculated as the ratio of the eligible room rent to the actual room rent charged.

## Section 4 Administrative and Process Terms

Waiting Period: a period from the policy start date during which a claim for a specific condition is not payable. Health Gold has an initial waiting period for all illnesses, specific waiting periods for certain conditions, and a Pre-Existing Disease waiting period. The applicable waiting period for a specific condition is stated in the Policy Wording version that governs the policy.

Portability: the right of an Insured Person to transfer health insurance cover from one registered insurer to another without losing credit for waiting periods already served. To avail of portability, the application must be submitted to the new insurer at least 45 days before the renewal date of the existing policy.

Grace Period: a period of 30 days after policy expiry during which the renewal premium may still be paid. No cover is available for losses occurring during the grace period.

Grievance: a formal written complaint submitted by a Policyholder or Insured Person to the Company's Grievance Redressal Officer. The Company must acknowledge a grievance within 3 working days and resolve it within 14 days of receipt.

Insurance Ombudsman: an independent dispute resolution authority established by the National Insurance Regulator to resolve disputes between policyholders and insurers at no cost to the policyholder. The Ombudsman is available as an escalation option if the Company's internal grievance process does not result in a satisfactory resolution.
