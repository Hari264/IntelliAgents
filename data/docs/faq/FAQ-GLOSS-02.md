## Section 1 What Do Common Claims Process Terms Mean

Claim Reference Number: a unique alphanumeric identifier assigned to a claim at the point of registration in the claims management system. The claim reference number must be quoted in all communications and document submissions relating to that claim.

Date of Loss: the date on which the medical event that led to the hospitalisation or treatment occurred. For the purpose of determining the applicable policy wording version and calculating waiting periods, the date of loss is typically the date on which the Insured Person first sought or received medical attention for the condition, not necessarily the date of admission.

Date of Discharge: the date on which the Insured Person was formally discharged from the Hospital following an in-patient admission. The date of discharge determines: (a) the end of the post-hospitalisation window; and (b) the start of the 30-day period within which reimbursement claim documents must be submitted.

Deficiency Notice: a written communication issued by the claims team to the Policyholder or claimant identifying documents that are required but have not yet been received. All missing documents must be listed in a single consolidated deficiency notice rather than in multiple separate requests, in accordance with CIR-05.

Last Necessary Document: the final document required by the insurer to assess a claim. The settlement timeline begins on the date the last necessary document is received. Where a document request is outstanding, the settlement clock is paused until the document is provided.

## Section 2 What Do Financial Assessment Terms Mean

Eligible Amount: the amount the Company will pay for a claim after applying all relevant policy terms — including the sum insured, applicable sub-limits, the room rent limit, any proportionate deduction and applicable exclusions. The eligible amount may be less than the amount claimed.

Disallowance: a decision to exclude a specific line item or amount from the settlement. Each disallowance must be explained in the settlement letter with the specific policy clause on which it is based.

Medical Necessity Assessment: the process of verifying that a treatment, procedure or hospitalisation was Medically Necessary as defined in Clause 1.2.3 of the applicable Policy Wording. Where medical necessity is in doubt, the claims team may seek an independent medical opinion at the Company's expense.

Settlement Letter: the written document issued to the Policyholder confirming the amount paid, the breakdown of the settlement and any items that were disallowed, with the applicable policy clauses. The settlement letter must be issued for every reimbursement claim, whether the claim is settled in full, partially or declined.

## Section 3 What Do Timeline and Process Terms Mean

Settlement Timeline: the regulatory period within which the Company must settle a claim, as defined in CIR-01. The standard settlement period is 30 days from receipt of the last necessary document; this is extended to 45 days where an investigation is required. Settlement outside these periods may attract interest on the amount payable.

Turnaround Time (TAT): the time taken to complete a specific process step within the claims cycle. TAT is monitored by the Company under SOP-TAT-01 and must not exceed the thresholds prescribed by the NIR. Key TAT milestones include the cashless pre-authorisation decision (1 hour) and the final discharge authorisation (3 hours).

Condonation: the act of accepting a late submission — such as a reimbursement claim submitted after 30 days of discharge — where the claimant provides a satisfactory written explanation for the delay. Condonation is at the Company's discretion and is not guaranteed.

Acknowledgement Period: the regulatory period within which the Company must acknowledge a Policyholder's grievance in writing. The acknowledgement period is 3 working days from the date of receipt of the grievance, as required by CIR-03.

## Section 4 What Do Regulatory and Administrative Terms Mean

National Insurance Regulator (NIR): the fictional regulatory authority for insurance in the setting of this corpus. The NIR issues circulars and guidelines that govern how health insurers process claims, communicate with policyholders, maintain networks and handle grievances. NIR circulars are summarised internally in the CIR series.

Circular: a regulatory communication issued by the NIR setting out requirements that all registered insurers must comply with. Internal summaries of relevant NIR circulars are in the CIR series (CIR-01 through CIR-06). Circulars do not form part of the policy wording but govern the Company's processes.

Policy Endorsement: a formal document issued by the Company that modifies the terms of an existing policy. Endorsements may add or remove riders, change the sum insured, correct details on the Policy Schedule or impose an exclusion following an underwriting review.

Reinstatement: the restoration of a policy that has lapsed owing to non-payment of the renewal premium within the grace period. Reinstatement is not automatic; it requires the Policyholder to pay any outstanding premium and submit a satisfactory declaration of continued good health. All waiting periods restart from the reinstatement date.
