## Section 1 How Do I Notify the Company of a Hospitalisation

Q: When and how must I notify the Company?

A: Under Clause 6.1 of the Policy Wording, the notification timing depends on whether the admission is planned or an emergency. For a planned hospitalisation, the Company must be notified at least 48 hours before the date of admission. For an emergency hospitalisation, notification must reach the Company within 24 hours of admission. Where the Insured Person's condition prevents timely notification, a family member or the treating hospital may notify on their behalf.

Notification may be made through the Company's helpline, email, online portal or third-party administrator, as specified in the Policy Schedule. A claim reference number will be issued on receipt of notification.

Q: What happens if I notify late?

A: Late notification does not automatically result in a rejection. The Company may condone a delay if there is sufficient cause. The Insured Person should provide a written explanation for the delay. The adjuster assesses the explanation and makes a decision on condonation under the applicable SOP. A claim will not be declined solely on late notification without giving the Insured Person an opportunity to explain.

## Section 2 What Documents Do I Need to Submit for Reimbursement

Q: What documents are required for a reimbursement claim?

A: All reimbursement claim documents must be submitted within 30 days of the date of discharge (Clause 6.2.1). The mandatory documents for all claims are: completed and signed claim form; original discharge summary; original itemised final bill; all original bills, receipts and payment proofs; all diagnostic test reports and prescriptions; a copy of the Insured Person's valid photo identity document; and a cancelled cheque or bank account details for electronic fund transfer.

For surgical claims, post-operative notes and the implant invoice with the implant sticker must also be submitted. For joint replacement claims, a pre-operative X-ray or MRI report and the orthopaedic surgeon's advice letter are additionally required.

Q: What if I cannot submit documents within 30 days?

A: The Company may accept late submissions if the Insured Person provides a satisfactory written explanation for the delay. Contact the Company as soon as possible and request a condonation review.

## Section 3 How Long Will My Claim Take to Process

Q: How long does claim settlement take?

A: Under NIR guidelines, all health insurance claims must be settled within 30 days of receipt of the last necessary document. Where an investigation is required, settlement must be completed within 45 days of receipt of the last necessary document.

Q: How quickly is a cashless pre-authorisation decision issued?

A: The Company must issue the cashless pre-authorisation decision within 1 hour of receiving the complete pre-authorisation request. The final discharge authorisation must be issued within 3 hours of receiving the hospital's final bill. These timelines are mandated by the National Insurance Regulator.

Q: What if the Company is late in settling my claim?

A: Where the Company fails to settle within the prescribed period, it is liable to pay interest at 2% above the prevailing bank rate for the period of delay. This interest is included in the settlement payment automatically where a breach has occurred.

## Section 4 What Can I Do If My Claim Is Declined

Q: What does a rejection letter contain?

A: Under NIR guidelines, the Company must communicate the reasons for a rejection in writing within 15 days of receiving the last necessary document. The rejection letter must cite the specific policy clause, state the claim reference and provide the contact details of the Grievance Redressal Officer.

Q: How do I challenge a rejection?

A: You may submit a written grievance to the Company's Grievance Redressal Officer. The Company will acknowledge the grievance within 3 working days and resolve it within 14 days. If you remain dissatisfied after the internal resolution, or if the grievance is not resolved within the 14-day period, you may escalate to the Insurance Ombudsman having jurisdiction over your place of residence. The Company does not charge any fee for processing a grievance.
