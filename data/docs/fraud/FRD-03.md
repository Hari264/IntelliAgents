## Section 1 Purpose

This document forms part of the Fraud Playbook series and is Restricted to claims adjusters and SIU staff. It defines the procedure to be followed when a health insurance claim has been referred for SIU investigation, whether through a mandatory referral threshold defined in FRD-01 Section 2 or through the red-flag scoring process in FRD-01 Section 3.

The investigation procedure must be applied consistently to all referred claims. The objective of the investigation is to gather and assess evidence that either supports or contradicts the legitimacy of the claim, and to reach a documented conclusion within the regulatory settlement timeline. Investigations must be conducted with professionalism and impartiality; no conclusion should be reached before the evidence has been fully reviewed.

All investigation activities and outcomes are subject to the confidentiality requirements in FRD-01 Section 4.

## Section 2 Investigation Steps

Upon receipt of a referral through the SIU module, the assigned SIU officer must follow the steps set out in this section.

2.1 Case intake: the SIU officer must acknowledge the referral within 1 working day of receipt and confirm the referral basis in the case file. Where the referral was triggered by a mandatory threshold (FT-01 to FT-04 as defined in FRD-01 Section 2), the specific threshold must be recorded. Where the referral was triggered by the scoring system, the red-flag score and band must be confirmed.

2.2 Initial document review: the SIU officer must review all documents in the claim file within 2 working days of case intake. The review covers the clinical records, itemised billing, policy documents, proposal form and prior claim history for the Insured Person.

2.3 Additional record requests: where the documents in the claim file are insufficient for a complete assessment, the SIU officer may request from the treating hospital: operation theatre logs; pharmacy dispensing records; nursing observation charts; admission and discharge register entries; and any other record relevant to verifying the hospitalisation. Requests must be framed as routine claims verification and must not disclose the investigative context.

2.4 Medical panel review: where the clinical evidence requires specialist interpretation, the SIU officer may refer the medical aspects of the case to the Company's independent medical panel. The panel's opinion is advisory; the final investigation conclusion remains the SIU officer's responsibility.

2.5 Telephonic verification: the SIU officer may conduct a verification call with the Insured Person or a family member to gather factual information about the hospitalisation. The call must not disclose the investigative nature of the contact. Refer to FRD-01 Section 4 for the required customer communication approach.

2.6 Decision timeline: the SIU officer must reach and record a recommendation within the earlier of: 30 days from the referral date; or the regulatory settlement deadline applicable to the claim. Where the investigation cannot be concluded within the regulatory timeline, the Head of SIU must be notified to determine next steps.

## Section 3 Field Visits

A field visit is a direct, in-person visit to the treating hospital conducted by the SIU officer or an authorised field agent engaged by the Company.

3.1 Mandatory trigger: a field visit is mandatory for any claim at a hospital on the Company's internal provider watchlist (FT-04 in FRD-01 Section 2). The field visit must be completed before cashless approval is issued for a watchlisted provider or before a reimbursement claim is settled.

3.2 Discretionary trigger: a field visit may be conducted at the SIU officer's discretion in any case where on-site verification is required, including cases where billing red flags identified in FRD-02 cannot be resolved through document review alone.

3.3 Field visit objectives: the field agent must verify: (a) that the hospital is operational and equipped for the type of treatment claimed; (b) that the claimed patient was admitted on the dates stated in the claim, by inspecting the hospital's admission and discharge register; (c) that the treatment claimed was actually rendered, by reviewing operation theatre records, nursing notes and pharmacy records; and (d) the specific billing items or clinical concerns that triggered the visit.

3.4 Conduct of the visit: field agents must carry authorised Company identification and must conduct the visit professionally. The field agent must not represent the visit as an investigation. The field agent must not retain original hospital records; they may take certified copies or photographs of records with the hospital's permission.

3.5 Field visit report: within 5 working days of the visit, the field agent must submit a written report to the SIU case file, recording: the date, time and location of the visit; the names and designations of hospital staff who provided access to records; the records reviewed; the specific verification objectives and what was found against each; and an overall summary of the field agent's findings.

## Section 4 Outcome Recording

Every investigation must conclude with a written outcome record that is stored in the Restricted SIU case file and summarised in the claims management system.

4.1 Proceed to full settlement: where the investigation does not identify grounds for declining or adjusting the claim, the SIU officer must document the conclusion with supporting reasoning and instruct the claims adjuster to settle the claim in the normal course. No reference to the investigation is to be made in any customer-facing communication.

4.2 Proceed to adjusted settlement: where billing anomalies are identified but the hospitalisation itself is genuine, the SIU officer must document the adjusted payable amount with a line-by-line explanation. The claim is settled at the adjusted amount. The settlement letter cites the applicable policy clause for any disallowed items and does not reference the investigation.

4.3 Decline with documented grounds: where the investigation identifies grounds for declining the claim, the SIU officer must prepare a written investigation report setting out: the specific evidence reviewed; the grounds for decline; and the applicable policy clause under which the decline is made. The decline is communicated to the Policyholder through SOP-REJCOMM-01, citing only the applicable policy clause, without reference to the investigation process.

4.4 Referral for legal action: where the investigation concludes that a claim is fraudulent and the evidence meets the threshold for legal referral, the SIU officer must escalate to the Head of SIU and the Legal function. All original documents and investigation records must be preserved.

4.5 All investigation outcomes, regardless of conclusion, must be recorded in the SIU module with the outcome date, outcome type, SIU officer's name and a brief summary. These records are confidential and are not accessible to standard claims processing staff.
