## Section 1 Purpose

This document forms part of the Fraud Playbook series and is Restricted to claims adjusters and SIU staff. It defines the billing-related red flags that must be checked when reviewing hospitalisation claims, the criteria that trigger a billing audit, and the process for managing the Company's internal provider watchlist.

Hospital billing fraud is one of the most common forms of health insurance fraud. It includes the inflation of legitimate claims through upcoding, unbundling and phantom charges, as well as the submission of entirely fabricated bills. Adjusters must be alert to the indicators described in this document and must escalate cases in accordance with FRD-01 and SOP-FRAUDREF-01.

This document must not be shared with any external party. Refer to FRD-01 Section 4 for confidentiality rules.

## Section 2 Billing Red Flags

The following are billing red flags that indicate a claim may warrant enhanced review. Adjusters must check for these indicators on every claim before finalising the assessment.

2.1 Tariff excess — FT-03: the billed amount for any procedure or for the total claim exceeds 150% of the Company's current network tariff for that procedure. When this threshold is met, a billing audit must be initiated. The adjuster must request the original billing records, theatre logs and pharmacy records from the hospital and compare each line item against the applicable tariff schedule. The billing audit outcome must be documented in the claim file.

2.2 Duplicate line items: the same treatment, diagnostic test or pharmaceutical item appears more than once in the itemised bill without clinical justification. Each duplicate must be identified and the hospital asked to confirm the basis for the repeated charge.

2.3 Unbundling: charges that should be covered within a surgical package rate are separately itemised to inflate the total bill. For example, anaesthesia, operation theatre charges and post-operative nursing may be billed separately when a standard surgical package is the appropriate tariff category.

2.4 Services not rendered: the itemised bill includes charges for procedures, tests or medicines that do not appear in the discharge summary, post-operative notes, nursing notes or pharmacy records. Any item billed but not reflected in the clinical records must be queried.

2.5 Upcoding: a procedure is billed under a more complex or higher-tariff category than the clinical records support. For example, a minor surgical procedure is billed as a major surgical procedure.

2.6 Inconsistent admission dates: the admission or discharge date on the bill does not match the dates in the discharge summary or the admission register. Any date discrepancy must be clarified with the hospital before settlement.

## Section 3 Provider Watchlist

The Company maintains an internal provider watchlist. The watchlist records hospitals and medical providers against which specific billing or clinical concerns have been identified through prior investigations or peer review.

3.1 Watchlist trigger — FT-04: when a cashless pre-authorisation request or a reimbursement claim is received for treatment at a hospital that appears on the watchlist at the relevant date, a field visit must be conducted before cashless approval is issued or before the reimbursement claim is settled. Refer to FRD-03 Section 3 for the field visit procedure.

3.2 Checking the watchlist: adjusters must check the watchlist in the SIU module of the claims management system at the date of pre-authorisation request (for cashless claims) or claim receipt (for reimbursement claims). Watchlist status may change month to month; the status at the relevant date governs.

3.3 Watchlist does not equal fraud: a hospital's presence on the watchlist means that enhanced scrutiny is required for claims at that provider, not that every claim at that provider is fraudulent. The field visit and enhanced review are the mechanisms through which the specific claim is assessed.

3.4 Confidentiality: the existence of the watchlist and the identity of watchlisted providers must not be disclosed to any external party, including the hospital itself. Adjusters must not inform a hospital that it is on the watchlist at any stage.

3.5 Watchlist updates: hospitals may be added to or removed from the watchlist by the SIU. The watchlist is reviewed and updated monthly. Adjusters who identify billing patterns or concerns that may warrant a hospital being added to the watchlist must refer the information to the SIU through the internal concern referral process.

## Section 4 Documentation

All billing red flags identified, billing audits conducted and watchlist checks performed must be documented completely in the claim file.

4.1 For each billing red flag identified, the adjuster must record: the specific flag; the date of identification; the evidence supporting the identification; the hospital's response (if a query was raised); and the final action taken.

4.2 For billing audits triggered by FT-03, the audit documentation must include: the tariff schedule used for comparison; a line-by-line comparison of billed amounts and tariff amounts; the total overbillings identified; and the adjusted payable amount. The audit outcome drives the settlement quantum, and the adjuster must not settle at the billed amount when the audit identifies a material overcharge.

4.3 For all watchlist checks, the adjuster must record the check date, the watchlist status found, and whether a field visit was triggered. A claim settled at a watchlisted provider without a field visit, other than where the SIU has specifically waived the requirement in writing, is a process breach.
