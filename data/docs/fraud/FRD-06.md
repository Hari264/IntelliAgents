## Section 1 Purpose

This document forms part of the Fraud Playbook series and is Restricted to claims adjusters and SIU staff. It defines the process for coordinating the handover of a claim from the claims adjuster to the SIU, for managing status updates during the investigation, and for closing cases and capturing lessons learned. It supplements FRD-01 (referral thresholds and red-flag scoring), FRD-02 (billing red flags and watchlist), FRD-03 (investigation procedure), FRD-04 (customer communication restrictions) and FRD-05 (case documentation). The referral thresholds that trigger SIU involvement are as defined in FRD-01; this document does not introduce new thresholds or scoring criteria.

## Section 2 Handover to the SIU

2.1 When a claim meets one or more of the referral thresholds in FRD-01, the claims adjuster must submit the referral through the SIU module in the claims management system without delay. The referral must include: the claim reference; the specific threshold or thresholds met (referencing FRD-01); the red-flag score where applicable; a brief description of the concern; and all claim documents assembled to date.

2.2 On receipt of the referral, the SIU must acknowledge it within the same working day and assign it to an SIU officer. The SIU officer's name must be recorded in the SIU module under the case file opened in accordance with FRD-05.

2.3 Once a case has been formally handed over to the SIU, the original claims adjuster must not independently continue assessing or communicating about the claim except under the direction of the SIU officer. All customer contacts must follow the restrictions in FRD-04.

2.4 Where the original adjuster has already issued a cashless pre-authorisation or begun the reimbursement assessment, the SIU officer must decide promptly whether to pause the assessment or allow it to continue under enhanced monitoring. The SIU officer must communicate this decision to the adjuster in writing via the SIU module.

## Section 3 Status Updates

3.1 The SIU officer must provide a written status update to the Head of SIU and to the original adjuster's supervisor at regular intervals throughout the investigation. The frequency of updates is determined by the Head of SIU based on the complexity of the case and the regulatory settlement clock.

3.2 Status updates must include: a summary of investigation actions taken since the last update; any new evidence obtained; any change in the risk assessment since the referral; and the expected timeline for the investigation conclusion.

3.3 Where the investigation is likely to cause a settlement delay that may result in interest liability under CIR-01, the Head of SIU must notify the Head of Claims immediately so that appropriate action can be taken.

3.4 The adjuster must not share status updates with the Policyholder, Insured Person or hospital beyond the approved holding statement in FRD-04 Section 3, regardless of how the investigation is progressing.

## Section 4 Closure and Lessons Learned

4.1 Every SIU case must be formally closed in the SIU module with a written closure note. The closure note must state: the final outcome of the investigation (proceed to settlement, adjust and settle, or decline with grounds); the basis for the conclusion; and any action taken or recommended against the hospital, provider or Insured Person.

4.2 Where the investigation concludes that the claim is genuine, the SIU officer must instruct the adjuster to proceed to settlement as soon as possible to minimise any further delay. The settlement communication to the Policyholder must not reference the investigation.

4.3 Where the investigation concludes that the claim should be declined, the SIU officer must prepare a written recommendation to the Head of Claims with the supporting evidence. The decline must be communicated to the Policyholder citing the applicable policy clause, in accordance with SOP-REJCOMM-01, and must not reference the investigation.

4.4 After closure, the SIU officer must complete a brief lessons-learned note for the case, identifying any pattern or technique that informed the concern, any process improvement that could prevent recurrence, and whether the case should be shared anonymously with the wider claims team as a training example. Lessons-learned notes are retained in the case file and reviewed by the Head of SIU quarterly.
