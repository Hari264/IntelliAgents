## Section 1 Purpose

This SOP defines the process for verifying whether a claimed condition constitutes a Pre-Existing Disease (PED) under Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd. health insurance policies, and for handling cases of suspected non-disclosure of a PED at the proposal stage.

Under the applicable Policy Wording, a Pre-Existing Disease is any condition, ailment, injury or disease that was diagnosed, or for which the Insured Person received medical advice or treatment, at any time during the 48-month period immediately before the date on which the Policy first incepted for that Insured Person (Clause 1.2.1). The PED waiting period applicable to a specific claim is governed by the wording version in force on the date of loss; refer to Clause 4.3 of the applicable policy wording version for the specific waiting period.

This SOP must be applied whenever a claimed condition may be linked to a PED, as identified during routine claim assessment or flagged by the claims adjuster or the medical assessor.

## Section 2 Indicators of PED

The following are indicators that a claimed condition may be a Pre-Existing Disease and warrant a PED verification as described in Section 3.

2.1 The Insured Person's prior medical records, discharge summary or treating physician's notes mention a pre-existing diagnosis, chronic condition or prior treatment for a condition that is the same as or related to the current claim.

2.2 The date of loss falls within the PED waiting period as per the policy wording version applicable (Clause 4.3), and the diagnosis is consistent with a chronic or progressive condition that is likely to have been present before policy inception.

2.3 The Insured Person declared one or more conditions on the proposal form, and the claimed condition appears to be related to or a complication of a declared condition.

2.4 The treating physician's notes or referral letter mention a history of the condition or a prior diagnosis that predates the policy start date.

2.5 The Insured Person had a prior health insurance policy with another insurer and was subject to waiting periods or loadings for a related condition, as evidenced by prior policy documents.

2.6 The clinical presentation or investigations suggest a chronic, degenerative or long-standing condition that is unlikely to have commenced after the policy start date.

## Section 3 Verification Steps

3.1 Review the proposal form for all conditions declared by the Insured Person at inception. Note any conditions disclosed with a diagnosis date or treatment history within the 48-month period before policy inception.

3.2 Request the complete inpatient and outpatient medical records for the Insured Person from the treating hospital for the period from at least 48 months before the policy start date to the date of loss. Where records are held by a prior treating physician, request those records as well.

3.3 Review the medical records against the definition of Pre-Existing Disease in Clause 1.2.1. Determine whether any mention of a diagnosis, investigation, prescription or treatment for the claimed condition (or a condition causally related to it) appears in the records from the 48-month lookback period.

3.4 Where the medical evidence is complex or the clinical link between a prior condition and the current claim is unclear, refer the matter to the Company's medical panel for an independent medical opinion. The opinion must address specifically whether the claimed condition constitutes a Pre-Existing Disease and whether the primary cause of the current claim is linked to a prior condition.

3.5 Document all findings in the claim file: the source of each piece of medical evidence reviewed, the date of each document, and the conclusion reached. If a PED is confirmed, record the specific clause under which the claim is assessed (Clause 4.3 of the applicable Policy Wording).

## Section 4 Non-disclosure Handling

Non-disclosure of a Pre-Existing Disease at the proposal stage is a serious matter that may affect the validity of the Policy and requires a separate underwriting review.

4.1 Where the verification in Section 3 reveals that the Insured Person had a condition that meets the definition of a Pre-Existing Disease under Clause 1.2.1 but did not declare it on the proposal form, the adjuster must flag the claim for non-disclosure review and notify the underwriting team.

4.2 The underwriting team will review the non-disclosure in accordance with UW-PED-01 and determine the appropriate course of action. Possible outcomes include: assessment of the claim on the basis that the PED waiting period as per the applicable Policy Wording applies; amendment of the policy terms at renewal; or, in cases of material non-disclosure, referral for possible policy cancellation in accordance with NIR guidelines as summarised in CIR-03.

4.3 Where the claim is placed on hold pending the non-disclosure review, the Policyholder must be informed in writing that the claim requires further assessment and that additional documents or information may be requested. Do not state the specific reason for the hold as non-disclosure at this stage; refer the wording of the communication to the team supervisor.

4.4 The adjuster must document all non-disclosure referrals in the claims management system, including the date of referral, the nature of the suspected non-disclosure and the underwriting team's decision. All non-disclosure cases must be reported in the monthly underwriting review.
