## Section 1 Purpose

This SOP governs the assessment and settlement of claims for Domiciliary Hospitalisation under health insurance policies issued by Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd. Under the applicable Policy Wording (Clause 2.4), Domiciliary Hospitalisation refers to medical treatment received by an Insured Person at their home in lieu of admission to a Hospital, where the treating physician certifies that hospitalisation was not possible or was medically inadvisable due to the nature of the Insured Person's condition. This SOP applies to all Domiciliary Hospitalisation claims across Health Gold and Health Silver policies.

## Section 2 Eligibility Checks

2.1 For a claim to qualify as Domiciliary Hospitalisation under the Policy Wording, all of the following conditions must be satisfied: (a) the period of continuous home-based treatment must exceed 3 consecutive days; (b) the treating physician must have certified in writing that hospitalisation was not possible or was medically inadvisable, with the specific clinical reasons stated; and (c) the nature and intensity of the home-based treatment must be equivalent to what would have been provided during in-patient hospitalisation.

2.2 Treatment that lasts 3 consecutive days or fewer does not qualify as Domiciliary Hospitalisation under the Policy Wording.

2.3 Routine outpatient consultations, home nursing visits for ongoing chronic condition management, and standard physiotherapy sessions do not qualify as Domiciliary Hospitalisation unless the treating physician's certification confirms that the specific criteria in Clause 2.4 of the applicable Policy Wording are met.

2.4 The adjuster must confirm that any applicable waiting period under the Policy Wording has been completed before proceeding to financial assessment.

## Section 3 Documents Required

3.1 In addition to the standard mandatory documents in SOP-DOCS-01, Domiciliary Hospitalisation claims require all of the following: (a) a written certification from the treating physician stating that hospitalisation was not possible or was medically inadvisable, with the specific clinical reasons; (b) daily treatment records for the entire duration of the home treatment period; (c) prescriptions for all medicines administered during the treatment period; and (d) nursing notes where a registered nurse provided care at the Insured Person's home.

3.2 All documents must be submitted within 30 days of the date on which the home treatment period concluded.

3.3 Where a registered nurse administered treatment, the nurse's current registration details must be provided alongside the nursing notes.

3.4 If the treating physician's certification refers to the Insured Person's condition but does not explicitly state that hospitalisation was inadvisable or not possible, the adjuster must request a supplementary written statement from the physician before the assessment can proceed.

## Section 4 Assessment Steps

4.1 Confirm eligibility using the criteria in Section 2 of this SOP before proceeding to financial assessment. Document the eligibility finding in the claims management system.

4.2 Review the treating physician's certification for completeness and clinical consistency. The certification must address both the need for home-based treatment and the specific reason hospitalisation was not possible or was medically inadvisable.

4.3 Review the daily treatment records to confirm that treatment continued for more than 3 consecutive days and that the intensity and type of care recorded is consistent with what would have been provided in a Hospital setting.

4.4 Review each claimed expense for medical necessity. Household items, food supplements and over-the-counter products that are not prescribed for a specific diagnosed condition being treated during the domiciliary period are not payable.

4.5 Apply applicable sub-limits and exclusions as stated in the Policy Wording. Record all findings and the basis for each decision in the claims management system before closing the assessment.
