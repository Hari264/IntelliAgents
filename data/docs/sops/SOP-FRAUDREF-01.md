## Section 1 Purpose

This SOP provides general guidance for claims adjusters and service staff on recognising when a health insurance claim may warrant a concern referral, and on the process for making that referral to the appropriate team within Aarogya Shield General Insurance Ltd.

This SOP provides general operational guidance only. Referral thresholds and red-flag scoring are defined in the Fraud Playbook, which is Restricted. They are not reproduced in this SOP. Adjusters who require access to the Fraud Playbook must request it through the Head of Claims. This SOP does not restrict the obligation to refer a concern — when in doubt, adjusters should refer rather than not refer, and the specialist team will assess the matter.

This SOP must be applied in a way that does not compromise ongoing reviews. In particular, the Insured Person or Policyholder must never be informed that a concern has been raised; refer to Section 4 for the required customer communication approach.

## Section 2 When to Raise a Concern

An adjuster should raise a concern when the claim presents one or more features that suggest the claim may not be genuine, the billed amounts may not be accurate, or the circumstances of the hospitalisation are unusual. The following are examples of the types of features that may warrant a referral. This list is illustrative and not exhaustive.

2.1 The claimed diagnosis is inconsistent with the clinical evidence in the discharge summary or the treating physician's notes.

2.2 The billing pattern from the hospital appears unusual — for example, identical itemised bills across multiple unrelated claims, or charges that appear disproportionate to the nature of the procedure and the class of hospital.

2.3 The hospitalisation appears to have been arranged primarily to generate a claim rather than for genuine medical treatment, based on the available clinical and administrative evidence.

2.4 The Insured Person has submitted multiple claims in a short period for different diagnoses from different hospitals, and the circumstances do not appear consistent with genuine illness.

2.5 The hospital or treating provider has been the subject of prior internal concerns, as communicated by the fraud and investigation team.

2.6 Documents submitted with the claim appear to have been altered or contain inconsistencies (for example, correction fluid on the bill, mismatched fonts, or signatures that differ between documents).

Referral thresholds and red-flag scoring are defined in the Fraud Playbook, which is Restricted. They are not reproduced in this SOP.

## Section 3 How to Refer

3.1 Do not delay processing the claim solely to gather more information before referring. Raise the concern as soon as the feature is identified, while the claim is at whatever stage it has reached.

3.2 Complete the internal concern referral form available in the claims management system. The form must include: the claim reference number; the policy number and Insured Person details; the specific feature or features that prompted the concern; a list of the documents in the claim file; and any prior claim history retrieved from the system.

3.3 Submit the referral form through the designated referral channel to the fraud and investigations team. The referral must be marked as confidential and must not be visible to general claims staff outside the referral workflow.

3.4 Do not communicate the referral to the treating hospital, the Insured Person, the Policyholder or any external party. Do not record the referral in any document that is accessible to the claimant or that could form part of a customer-facing communication.

3.5 Once the referral is submitted, log the claim status in the claims management system under rejection code CL-601 (Claim under additional review). All subsequent communications with the claimant must use the CL-601 approach described in Section 4. The settlement clock continues to run; if the concern cannot be resolved within the regulatory settlement timeline, consult the fraud and investigations team and the Head of Claims on next steps.

## Section 4 Customer Communication

Under no circumstances should an Insured Person, Policyholder or any representative be informed that a concern referral has been made or that a review of any kind related to the authenticity of the claim is in progress.

4.1 If the claimant contacts the Company to enquire about the status of their claim after a concern referral has been made, the service agent must use only the following approach: inform the customer that their claim is under additional review and that the Company will be in contact with them shortly. This is the script for rejection code CL-601 as set out in SCR-GEN-01. No further information is to be given regarding the nature of the review.

4.2 Do not use the words "fraud", "investigation", "suspicious" or any similar term in any oral or written communication with the claimant. Do not state the reason for the additional review, the identity of the team conducting the review or the likely timeline for completion.

4.3 If the claimant becomes persistent or hostile in their enquiry, escalate the call to the team supervisor. The supervisor must also follow the CL-601 communication approach and must not disclose any additional information about the nature of the review.

4.4 If the review concludes that the claim is payable, process settlement in the normal course and do not make any reference to the review having taken place. If the review results in a rejection, the rejection must be communicated on the applicable policy clause basis, following SOP-REJCOMM-01, without reference to the review process.
